Five controls, every deployment

This is what "governed" means in practice, not in a policy binder.

01 Trusted Data The model only ever sees the client files it is scoped to touch, nothing from an engagement it has no letter for.
02 Right-Sized Access A model reading tax workpapers cannot also reach a separate client's audit files.
03 Guardrails Hard limits on what the model can draft unsupervised versus what always routes to a human first.
04 Human Escalation A named partner or manager reviews anything that leaves the guardrail, before it reaches a client.
05 Full Audit Trail Every draft, every review, every sign-off logged, so "who approved this" has a documented answer.

Models change quietly. Your controls should not.

A vendor updates a model and your drafting workflow behaves differently overnight. Nobody voted on that. Two things keep the ground steady.

In every build

Governance Rider

Every Operational Blueprint contract includes a rider that names what happens when a vendor deprecates or retrains a model your firm depends on. It is written down before you need it, not negotiated after.

In every quarter

Covered Hours

Governance-as-a-Service includes covered hours each quarter specifically for re-checking controls against whatever the model vendors changed since the last review.

What happens if our vendor deprecates the model our workflow was built on?

Your governance rider or covered hours (whichever applies) trigger a re-audit of that workflow before the deprecation date. We re-scope the controls against the replacement model rather than letting staff quietly adapt on their own.

What if a model's behavior drifts between updates, even without a formal deprecation?

Drift shows up first in the review gate, drafts that used to pass clean start needing more correction. That is a signal we build into the audit trail, not something staff are left to notice on their own and quietly work around.

How do you handle new state or federal rules on AI use in client work?

New regulation gets mapped against your existing controls under GaaS, and flagged to counsel for review before it becomes a compliance gap instead of a planning item.

Straight answers

What does an AI audit for a professional services firm actually check?

It is an 82-point diagnostic across six dimensions: business context, technology maturity, automation opportunities, data infrastructure, decision authority, and investment readiness. For a CPA firm, consultancy, agency, or engineering firm, that means mapping which tools touch client data, who signs off before model output reaches a deliverable, and where an engagement letter is silent on AI use.

Does this cover GLBA Safeguards and AICPA confidentiality requirements?

Yes. The audit maps AI touchpoints against the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct confidentiality rule, peer review documentation standards, and the GLBA Safeguards Rule where client financial data is in scope. You get a rubric your counsel can review, not a generic checklist.

Our engagement letters do not mention AI. Is that a real exposure?

Yes. If a letter is silent on AI use and a model touches client data or drafts part of the work product, the client learns about it after the fact if at all. That is an E&O conversation waiting to happen. The audit flags every engagement type where this gap exists.

How is this different from a generic AI policy template?

A template tells staff what not to do. The audit tells you where your actual exposure sits: which tools your people already use, which client data can reach them, and which deliverables carry model-drafted content nobody with a license reviewed. Then it builds controls around those specific gaps.

What does the engagement cost and how long does it take?

It is a fixed-phase, flat-fee engagement, scoped in one call once we understand your practice areas and headcount. No hourly surprises. Timelines and next steps come out of that first conversation.

If a partner had to sign off on every prompt today, could they?

That is the question the audit answers. Not whether your firm should use AI, but whether the judgment behind it is still yours.
