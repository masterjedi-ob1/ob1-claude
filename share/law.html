SHEET07 / 07 TITLESTRAIGHT ANSWERS SCALENTS DATE2026 DRAWN BYOB.1

Straight answers.

Does the AI audit require access to privileged files?

No. We audit how AI tools are configured, accessed, and supervised across your firm, not the content of client matters. Where a review step needs to touch anything sensitive, it is counsel-gated: your own lawyers control what we see, and the finished rubric is reviewed by counsel before it reaches you.

What does the audit actually score?

Six dimensions: business context, technology maturity, automation opportunity, data infrastructure, decision authority, and investment readiness. Eighty-two points across those six produce one readiness score and a ranked list of fixes.

We already have an AI use policy. Why do we need this?

A policy states intent. An audit tests practice against it: which associates are using which tools, on which matters, with what oversight. Most firms we meet have a policy and a gap between the policy and the desk.

How long does the engagement take, and what does it cost?

It is a fixed-phase, flat-fee engagement, scoped in one call. Most firms move from audit to a working control set inside a quarter.

Does this cover transactional practice, or only litigation risk?

Both. Hallucinated citations are the headline risk, but confidentiality under Rule 1.6, supervision under Rule 5.3, and work-product exposure apply to deal teams and diligence rooms the same as they apply to litigators.

If a client asked what your firm's AI policy actually catches, what would you show them?

A score, a ranked list of findings, and a plan reviewed by counsel. Scoped in one call.
